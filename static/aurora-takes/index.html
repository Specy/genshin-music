Aurora Voice Takes

In Sky, the Voice of AURORA plays one of several recorded takes each time you press a note, picked at random. Sky Music Nightly will use one fixed take per note, so they need choosing. Every take is here, cut from recordings of the game.

How to help

  1. Choose Suggested 1, 2 or 3 in the bar below and press Copy into my set.
  2. Go through the notes. Play each take and press Use for … on the one that fits best. Play pattern runs your set as a scale, chords or a melody.
  3. Mark takes that sound off with Reject, and ones you like with Keep.
  4. At the bottom, press Copy text and send it back to whoever shared this page with you.

Your picks stay in this browser until you send them. Headphones help.

How the takes were found

  • Each note was recorded in game with 22–30 presses. Presses were split on the rise out of the previous note's tail.
  • Two presses count as the same take when their waveforms stay correlated above 0.89 across the whole note after alignment. Different takes never exceeded 0.69, so the split has no borderline cases.
  • Repeats of a take are identical through the fade: same hold length to ±10 ms, same fade. The fade is part of the recording, not a reaction to the key.
  • Pitch is measured with YIN over the held part. Octave errors are folded, and cents are relative to A4 = 440 Hz.

Looping and holding

  • No take loops. Each is one sung vowel held 4–8 s, then a natural fade that reaches −50 dB by 9–11 s and digital silence at 11–11.9 s.
  • Fine loudness ripples never repeat inside a take (self-similarity 0.24–0.75, where a loop would read ≈1.0).

Where the current Aurora comes from

  • The Aurora in Sky Music Nightly today is built from an older sample rip recorded in D major and shifted down two semitones.
  • Of the takes on this page, only the three D4 ones are confirmed to be the same recordings as files in that rip.
