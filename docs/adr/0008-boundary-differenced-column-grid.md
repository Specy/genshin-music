# The composer plays on the song's own boundary grid

How long columns `[from, to)` last in ms had two answers. `ComposedSong.toRecordedSong` — what the player loads a composed song through, and what the midi and sheet exports are built from — accumulates EXACT per-column durations and rounds only where it emits a time, so a column's onset is `round(Σ msPerBeat × changer)`. The composer's own `columnsDurationMs` rounded EACH column and summed those, and `ComposerRenderer.columnDurationMs` rounded one column the same way for the playhead glide. The two policies agree only where `60000 / bpm × changer` is whole ms. Over 400 plain columns (changer 1) the composer's grid lands, against the conversion's: **0 ms at bpm 40, 60, 100, 120, 150, 200, 240 and 300; −182 ms at 110; +133 at 90; +171 at 140; +109 at 220** — one direction at some tempos, the other at others, and at bpm 220 (0.2727 ms gained per column) the accumulated policy is a full 1/8 beat out by column 125. What a user heard the composer play and what the same song exported (or re-imported) as therefore disagreed about where every column after the first begins.

The conversion is the truth side and does not move. Its exactness is what keeps a midi export landing on the grid a DAW re-imports it onto — accumulated rounding puts every later note a fraction of a column late and sprouts spurious sub-beat columns — and its output is pinned byte-for-byte by the golden fixtures. So we decided (2026-08-22): **the composer asks the song how long its columns last, and the song answers with differences of its own rounded cumulative boundaries.** `ComposedSong.columnsDurationMs(from, to)` is `round(prefix[to]) − round(prefix[from])`, where `prefix[i]` is the exact ms at which column `i` begins. It lives beside `toRecordedSong` for the reason it exists: the two must never drift, so they sit where one reader sees both.

Key choices within that:

- **Telescoping, not agreement by argument.** The transport's grid is a running sum of per-column answers. Summing `[0,1), [1,2), … [n−1,n)` collapses to `round(prefix[n])` — precisely the onset `toRecordedSong(0)` writes for column `n`. Alignment is a property of the arithmetic rather than something two call sites have to keep promising each other, and a spanned note's `columnsDurationMs(index, index + span)` is the same integer the conversion stores as that note's `duration`, exactly.
- **The prefix accumulates in the conversion's order.** Left to right, adding `msPerBeat × changer` per column, because `toRecordedSong`'s `exactTime` does. The two sums must be bit-identical, not merely equal in theory: `Math.round` decides on a half-ulp, so an algebraically equal sum reached in another order can land on the other side of a `.5` and round the opposite way.
- **A per-instance cache, validated on read against `(structureVersion, bpm)`.** Not invalidated by the mutators, because `bpm` is its own signal on `Song` and no bpm write passes through `#bumpStructure` — there is nothing for an invalidation hook to hang off. `structureVersion` is only comparable within one song instance, which a per-instance cache satisfies; reading both keys through their getters also leaves a reactive caller subscribed to exactly what the answer depends on. A rebuild is one pass over the columns and costs well under a millisecond even at 4000 columns.
- **The renderer takes the same numbers.** `ComposerRenderer.columnDurationMs` builds the identical grid over its own `state.columns` at `state.bpm` (cached against `(columns identity, structureVersion, bpm)`, the on-read pattern `maxSpan()` already uses) and differences it. Matching the transport's arithmetic is what keeps the glide the same length as the column it travels through; its `Math.max(1, …)` floor stays for inputs a difference can still be zero on — an index past the last column, or a column shorter than a millisecond.

Considered and rejected: **making `toRecordedSong` accumulate rounded durations instead** — it is the format's truth, the goldens pin it, and the drift it deliberately avoids is what breaks midi round-tripping; that comment in it is a defect report, not a preference. **Walking the columns per query** — correct, and unaffordable: `resync()` rebuilds the committed window on every note edit made during playback, and the window is queried per column committed, per spanned note (O(span) queries each) and per held key at every column boundary, so an O(n) walk per query makes each resync quadratic in the song's length rather than linear. **A bpm-free changer prefix multiplied at query time** — mathematically equal and cheaper to invalidate (bpm would leave the key), rejected for the half-ulp above; tempo changers are dyadic today (1, ½, ¼, ⅛), but they are game-config data and nothing keeps them so.

Consequences: `ComposedSong` gains a public read method and a private cache; `Composer.svelte`'s local `columnsDurationMs` becomes a one-line delegation, so the transport callback, `commitColumn`'s spanned notes, sustain-recording quantization and `pressSpansCoveringStart`'s resumed spans all move onto the song's grid together. `TransportCallbacks.columnDurationMs` no longer asks the caller for pre-rounded per-column values; it documents that it is handed boundary differences and does no rounding of its own. `test/columnTiming.test.ts` pins both halves — the arithmetic exhaustively across twelve tempos, and the real `ComposerTransport` driven over real songs — including a non-vacuity guard that the old policy really does disagree on the grid the test uses. No fixture changes, which is the point: nothing on the conversion side moved.
