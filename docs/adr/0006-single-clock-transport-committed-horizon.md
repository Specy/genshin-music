# Single-clock transport with committed horizon

The composer's playback loop timed audio from the main thread (`Date.now()` + `delayOffset` drift correction) and pushed each column's sound a user-configurable `lookaheadTime` into the future, which made the whole model — selected column, keyboard flash, sustain recording, end-of-song — run one lookahead ahead of what the listener heard. Every consumer then compensated: the renderer offset its glide segments by `+lookaheadMs`, pause parked the playhead backward with an end-of-song special case, sustain quantization used a midpoint rule because performers' presses landed near the end of the model's column, and audio export drained `lookahead + 1000` ms.

We decided: **one clock, two meanings of "ahead".** All playback timing lives on `AudioContext.currentTime`. A transport (`ComposerTransport`, the peer of `Metronome` and built on its proven pattern) commits column audio to the audio clock up to a fixed ~1s horizon — never less than the next 2 columns, so slow tempos can't starve the margin — while the sounding cursor (`song.selected` during playback) advances exactly at each column's audio-clock boundary. The scheduler's ahead-position is purely internal; nothing outside the transport ever sees it. The lookahead setting is deleted: accuracy no longer trades against responsiveness, so there is nothing left to configure.

Key choices within that:

- **Single loop, grid-derived sleeps.** One per-column loop both advances the sounding cursor and tops up the committed window. Each sleep is computed from the committed audio grid (`(audioTimeOf(next) − ctx.currentTime) × 1000`, worker-timers so background tabs keep playing), so cursor/audio drift is unrepresentable rather than corrected. `delayOffset` is deliberately not ported — see the warning in `Metronome.ts`. Column audio times are anchored (`previous + duration`), never re-derived from "now", so top-up seams cannot jitter.
- **Resync on mutation, not staleness.** Any change to what-should-sound (note add/remove/span, BPM, tempo changer, mute, instrument swap, a jump of `selected`, stop) cancels every committed-but-unstarted event and recommits from the sounding position. This is what makes the 1s horizon free: it behaves identically to a 0ms window from the user's point of view. Stop needed cancellation anyway — with a 1s window an uncancellable pause would leak a full second of runaway notes.
- **Instruments own cancellation.** One-shot `AudioBufferSourceNode`s were fire-and-forget and uncancellable; each `Instrument` now keeps a registry of not-yet-started events with `cancelScheduledAfter(audioTime)`, and `play`/`pressNote` take an absolute audio time instead of a relative delay. Committed audio is retractable until it starts; once sounding it always rings out (deleting a note never silences its in-flight sound — the rule that was previously accidental).

Considered and rejected: keeping `selected` as the scheduler cursor and shifting every consumer (that is the compensation regime being deleted); accepting in-window staleness (still requires the cancellation registry for stop, so it saves nothing); a separate UI-follower process synced to the scheduler (two things to keep from drifting — collapsed into the single loop above).

Consequences: `ComposerSettings.settingVersion` bumps (one-time settings reset — the scheme's routine cost); the renderer's `+lookaheadMs` offsets, backward pause-parking rationale, and quantize midpoint justification are deleted rather than adjusted; live recording gets _more_ accurate because a performer playing with what they hear writes into the correct column by construction. Scope at the time was the composer only: the Player's `playSong` loop (`PlayerKeyboard.svelte`) still used the wall-clock idiom with zero lookahead, and was named here as an explicit future adopter — the Instrument-level absolute-time API and cancellation registry were already player-agnostic. That adoption happened in ADR-0009, on a lean sibling transport over a flat event timeline; the player is no longer a wall-clock surface.
