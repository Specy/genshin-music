# MIDI import chooses its own instruments, and drops what none of them can play

The MIDI importer never decided what a song was played on: it mapped track _i_ onto layer _i_ of whatever song happened to be open (`defaultLayerForTrack`, clamped to that song's roster), so the timbre of an import was an accident of the previous edit. Two consequences followed. A foreign file landed on instruments nobody chose — a drum track on a Lyre, four melodic tracks stacked on one layer of a single-layer song — and the only repair was to build the roster by hand first and re-import. And the panel's own controls disagreed about which roster they meant: the conversion read the file's metadata roster when the file was one of ours and the open song's otherwise (`MidiParser.svelte`'s `layers`), while the per-track dropdown always listed the open song's, so a metadata file with more layers than the open song offered a `<select>` with no matching option and reassigned tracks into the wrong roster on any touch. Note policy was inherited rather than chosen too: a number outside the Song Grid's `MIDI_BOUNDS` was dropped, and a number inside the grid that the landing instrument simply had no button for was kept, silent and dimmed — so what survived an import depended on which of two unrelated ranges a note fell outside of. Meanwhile every settings change re-ran the conversion and replaced the whole song through `loadSong`, which meant any edit made while the panel was open was destroyed by the next keystroke in it, and the panel's own base-pitch selector transposed the open song's notes before any conversion had happened at all.

We decided (2026-08-24): **the import chooses the instrument per track, builds the song's roster from those choices, and excludes every note the chosen instrument cannot voice unless asked to keep it.** The per-track control is an instrument, not a layer: it is pre-selected with a Suggested Instrument derived from the track's General MIDI identity, and the generated song's roster is exactly one layer per selected track, in file order, carrying that track's instrument and Basepoint. What "out of range" means follows from that choice rather than from the grid: a note is out of range when the track's instrument cannot voice it at its effective Basepoint — a Stranded Note, by the name the rest of the app already uses — and the importer removes it by default so that every note in an imported song sounds. `Include out of range notes` keeps them instead, visible and dimmed, with one exception nothing can rescue: a number outside the Addressable Span is excluded either way, because no instrument at any Basepoint can voice it however the song is later edited. And because the preview IS the open song, the composer is read-only while the importer is open.

Key choices within that:

- **Excluded by default, and it is the opposite of ADR-0011's default.** Four days ago cross-game conversion chose as-is over folded: what does not fit is marked and kept, never silently rewritten. This decides the reverse for the sibling path, and the difference is where in a song's life the two sit. Conversion rewrites a song that already exists and has been saved, so a loss it applies is unrecoverable and unasked for; import is authoring at the door, where nothing has been stored, the source file is still on disk, and the toggle governing the loss is on screen next to the count of what it costs. The two rules agree on the thing that matters — neither ever moves a note to make it fit.
- **The suggestion reads the file's General MIDI identity, never the track's notes.** Four tiers: the GM patch name against each instrument's `midiName`, then the GM family against each instrument's `family`, then the nearest family in a fixed General-MIDI adjacency order that this game has any instrument for, then the game's default; ties within a tier go to the earliest instrument in the game's menu roster, which is already authored melody-first and SFX-last in both games. Scoring the track's actual notes would pick better instruments and would also move the pre-selection under the user's hand every time the offset or a Basepoint changed, because the same notes strand differently at every transposition. A suggestion that jumps while you are reading it is worse than one that is merely imperfect, and every tier's answer is one dropdown click from being overruled.
- **`midiName` and `family` are validated separately, and are not required to agree.** They do different jobs: `midiName` is the program number MIDI export writes, and six instruments declare a string General MIDI does not have — `@tonejs/midi`'s name setter takes `indexOf`, gets `-1`, assigns nothing, and the track exports as patch 0, an acoustic grand piano. That is a live export bug and every one of the six is corrected. `family` is the suggestion key, and four instruments declare a word that is not a GM family at all (`vocal`, `Bass`, `percussion`, `synth`); those are corrected too. The four instruments whose valid `family` merely disagrees with the family their patch number implies are left exactly as authored — General MIDI files harmonica under _organ_, and a curated answer of `reed` is a better key for choosing an instrument by ear than the accident of GM's block layout.
- **A percussion track is answered by its channel, not by its name.** `@tonejs/midi` reports the family of a channel-9 track as the literal `drums`, which is not one of the sixteen GM families, and its `name` from a nine-entry sparse kit map that is `undefined` for most patch numbers. Both of the first two tiers miss it entirely. Drum tracks are the commonest non-melodic track in real files, so `drums` enters the adjacency order as a first-class entry resolving to the game's percussive roster, and every tier is written to survive an absent patch name.
- **The floor is the Addressable Span, and it moves into `noteIds`.** `addressableSpan()` — the lowest Sounding Pitch any instrument has at Basepoint C, up to the highest lifted by the highest Basepoint — already existed for the Pro View's axis, in a UI-tier file the core-tier importer cannot import. It relocates to `$core/Songs/noteIds`, beside the sounding tables it is derived from, so the axis the Pro View draws and the floor the importer refuses to cross cannot disagree about what the game can address.
- **The lock is enforced in the handlers, not in the markup.** Roughly half the note-entry surface reaches the song without touching a control a `disabled` prop could block: physical note keys, MIDI note-on/off, Pro View cell taps, canvas long-press and its drag, and the sustain recorder's per-tick span growth. The subtlest is the Duration Hold, which turns a plain selection move — which stays unlocked — into a span write, so opening the importer settles or abandons the gestures already in flight rather than only refusing new ones. Disabled controls are what the lock looks like; guards in the mutating functions are what it is.
- **The importer's base-pitch selector stops transposing the open song.** It called through to the composer's own pitch funnel, which rewrote every note of the loaded song, counted a change and could autosave it — before any conversion had run. Blocking that call under the lock would leave the selector dead; the call is deleted instead, because `loadSong` already re-seeds the composer's pitch setting from each preview song it installs.

Considered and rejected: **keeping stranded notes by default, as today** — it is the status quo and it is honest about the file, but a note the chosen instrument cannot voice is invisible in the Compressed View and inaudible everywhere, so the default would keep producing songs whose note count and sound disagree with no indication of which notes are which; **deriving `family` from `midiName` and deleting the field** — one source instead of two, at the cost of making Sky's only `organ` a harmonica and unfiling a tinkle bell from chromatic percussion; **suggesting the instrument from the track's notes** — better fits, and a pre-selection that moves whenever the transposition does; **per-track offsets in place of one global offset scored per track** — `localOffset` already exists for the user who wants it, and automating it transposes tracks relative to each other, which is the one edit that silently breaks an arrangement; **a detached read-only preview song instead of locking the live composer** — a second song to keep in step, and the canvas is the preview that makes the panel worth using; **regenerating the frozen config-surface fixture to absorb the corrected metadata** — the fixture is frozen precisely so a config edit cannot quietly redefine what the app used to be, and the sanctioned form is an explicit expected-deviation entry beside the existing `midiBounds.upper` one.

Consequences: `defaultLayerForTrack` is deleted, and the reasoning in its docstring — that our own export writes one MIDI track per layer including silent ones, so an own-file's track index addresses its metadata entry — moves into the importer's metadata lookup, which is now the only thing that needs it. `MidiImportLayer` stops describing the open song's roster and starts describing the roster being built; `MidiImportOptions.layers` is derived from the panel's own selections. `suggestOffset` takes per-track instruments and Basepoints instead of a flat union of playable nominals, because a single global semitone can only be scored honestly once each track's own Basepoint is known. The octave-folding control aims at the chosen instrument's own span rather than at `MIDI_BOUNDS`; the existing fold loop is replaced with modulo arithmetic, and the fold is stated as best-effort — four Sky instruments span less than an octave, so no fold can always land inside them, and the voiceability gate is the decider. Six `midiName` corrections change the program numbers our MIDI exports write, which flips the golden byte fixtures for both games. `MIDI_BOUNDS` stops being the importer's discard rule and remains what it always was, the Song Grid's own extent. ADR-0007's deferral of accidental-preserving import is untouched and still open: import policy stays white-key, and this decision changes only which of those white keys survive. The composer gains a read-only mode with no other user of it yet; whether anything else should adopt one is left open deliberately.
