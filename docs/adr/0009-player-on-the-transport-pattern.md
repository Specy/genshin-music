# The player plays on the transport pattern too

ADR-0006 moved the composer onto one clock and left the player's `playSong` loop behind as the wall-clock idiom's last holdout, naming it an explicit future adopter. That loop timed a song from the main thread: `await delay(noteTime - previous + delayOffset)` per note, a `delayOffset` re-measured against `Date.now()` after every note, a `> 16` ms threshold below which a note simply fired without waiting at all, and a trailing `delay(finalReleaseTime - lastNoteTime)` standing in for the end of the song. Three costs followed from that, and they were not theoretical. Every note's trigger time was whatever the main thread got round to — a Pixi draw, a Svelte flush, a GC pause moved a note audibly, and the compensation term corrected when we WOKE while still firing the sound by hand. The audio instant and the UI instant were fused, so the keyboard flash, the sheet cursor and a live recording could only ever be as accurate as the trigger. And because every note reached `Instrument` at "now", song playback took the same-button retrigger choke and the 64-voice steal that exist for live presses — while the offline export, committing ahead of the render clock, deliberately does not: a dense song already rendered FULLER than the same song sounded live.

We decided (2026-08-22): **play mode adopts the transport pattern, over a flat event timeline planned by the export's own planner.** `PlayerTransport` (`src/lib/audio/PlayerTransport.ts`) commits events to `AudioContext.currentTime` up to the same ~1 s horizon, and a sounding cursor advances exactly at each event's audio-clock boundary. `planSongRender` — already the single answer to which tracks are audible, at what Basepoint, whether a note presses or plays, and for how long — becomes the input to live playback as well as to the export, so the two cannot disagree about what a song sounds like. Scope is PLAY MODE only: practice and approaching keep their tick/queue machinery, because their sounds are user presses on the immediate path and there is nothing for a committed horizon to be ahead of.

Key choices within that:

- **A lean sibling, not a generalization of `ComposerTransport`.** The two share the family's idioms (the injectable `TransportClock`/`TransportTimer`, worker-timers so a backgrounded tab keeps playing, absolute anchored times, sleeps aimed at the next boundary, deliberately no `delayOffset` — see Metronome's warning) and nothing else. The composer's transport is column-shaped, ADR-0008 pins its grid to the song's own boundary differences, and most of its size is resync machinery; a shared abstraction would have to carry all of that into a surface that needs none of it.
- **NO resync-on-mutation, deliberately.** A player run's song is immutable while it plays: no note edits, no roster changes, no tempo edits, and a speed change or a seek stops the run and anchors a fresh one. So there is no "the song changed under a running transport" case, and the whole rebuild path — the old-grid start map, the pending-anchor recreation, the two-phase catch-up — is absent by design rather than by omission. That absence is what keeps the class a fraction of its sibling's size.
- **The commit floor is TIME-based, not a count of events.** `ComposerTransport` keeps at least `TRANSPORT_MIN_COMMIT_AHEAD` columns committed past the cursor; a count says nothing here, because a chord is several events at one instant and "two events ahead" can be three notes of one chord and zero seconds of margin. What the floor has to guarantee is that the next sounding boundary is never uncommitted, so that is what it asks for: commit through `now + horizon`, and past it if necessary to reach the end of the next boundary.
- **`onSounding` fires for the anchor event, unlike the composer's anchor column.** There the anchor is a column the caller selected before asking to play from it, so echoing it back would only invite re-entrancy. Here it is the first note anybody hears, and its flash, its cursor position and its recording entry all belong to that instant.
- **Seek resumes covering spans**, on the composer's `pressSpansCoveringStart` rule: a note cut off before the start whose span still covers it is pressed at the anchor's own absolute audio time, entering its sample where the playhead would have reached and holding for what is left. One difference has to be stated — the composer leans on its no-overlap invariant to know the nearest earlier same-(track, id) note is the only possible coverer, and a RECORDED song may hold two notes of one id on one track at once. The backward scan's first sighting is taken as the answer: the nearest earlier note is the one the playhead is inside, and an older one still ringing underneath is a doubling nobody can pick out once resumed. One-shots are not resumed; their attack happened in the past.
- **Stop is three steps, in order.** The transport stops advancing, then the committed-but-unstarted window is retracted through every instrument's `cancelScheduledAfter`, then what is already sounding is faded. Skipping the middle step leaks up to a second of runaway notes — ADR-0006's exact rationale, and the reason the cancellation registry exists at all. Unmounting the keyboard runs the same retraction, and `Instrument.dispose` now retracts a window of its own: stopping a song swaps the song's instruments back to the user's own, and that disposal runs before the keyboard's debounced teardown, so the sweep never reaches the engines the notes were committed on. Disconnecting a disposed engine makes those notes inaudible, which is not the same as retracting them — measured in the headless run below, and fixed at the source rather than left to the disconnect.

Considered and rejected: **generalizing `ComposerTransport` to cover both** — it is column-shaped, resync-laden and pinned by ADR-0008 to the composer's own grid; parameterizing it would drag that into a surface with a flat timeline and an immutable song. **Scheduling ahead from inside the old loop** — keeping `delay()` for the wait and passing an absolute `at` to the instruments — that is two clocks again, with the wall clock deciding when to look and the audio clock deciding when to sound, which is precisely the compensation regime ADR-0006 deleted. **Feeding the transport the sliced note list directly instead of the plan** — cheaper, and it would give the player a second opinion about audibility, Basepoint and press-vs-play, which is how an export comes to differ from the performance it was taken from.

Consequences: `PlannedEvent` gains `noteIndex`, the way back from an event to the note of the song it came from, because the sheet cursor and chunk position are keyed by a note's index; the plan leaves an inaudible track's notes out entirely, so the player's counting catches up over the indexes it skips rather than counting events (a chunk that never sounds would otherwise leave the chunk cursor one behind for the rest of the song). Song notes no longer flow through `Player.playSound`, which becomes the live-press path alone, and `commitSongNote` is the whole song path; with it goes `Player.svelte`'s roster copy of the song's `InstrumentData`, which only that branch read — audibility and Basepoint are the plan's answers now. The recording hook moves with the sound it records, from trigger time to the sounding moment, so a recording made while a song plays writes what the performer heard. A muted or soloed-away track no longer flashes keys on the keyboard, which follows from the plan owning audibility. `delayOffset`, the `> 16` ms threshold, the per-note `delay()` and the trailing release delay are deleted; the mid-play speed-change restart stays and now stops and re-anchors. `ComposerTransport`'s docstring, `Metronome`'s warning and ADR-0006's closing sentence, all three of which named the player as a wall-clock holdout, are corrected to point here — the warnings themselves stand, they simply no longer have a live example to name. `test/playerTransport.test.ts` pins the transport against a manual clock and a fake timer, and the player's component tests drive the same two seams.
