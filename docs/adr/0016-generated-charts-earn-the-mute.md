# Generated charts earn the mute by covering the part

A vsrg song is a chart laid over an **audio song**: the background song ticks its own notes at play time and the player's presses sound the notes each Hit Object carries, with `trackModifiers[trackIndex].muted` deciding which background tracks stay silent (`vsrg-player/+page.svelte`). Every chart in the app has been hand-placed onto that structure one snap point at a time, and the pairing of "mute this instrument" with "chart what it was playing" has been an authoring convention held in the author's head — nothing in the model connects them, and nothing notices when a muted track's notes are only half charted. Automating chart creation forces the convention to become a rule, because a generator that thins notes for playability and mutes the track anyway does not produce an easier chart; it produces a song with holes in it, and does so silently, at exactly the difficulty aimed at beginners.

We decided (2026-08-30): **generation may drop any note it likes, but a background track is muted only when the chart covers every one of its notes.** Coverage is the whole contract — onset, pitch, and, on an instrument whose config declares `sustain`, the sustain too. A track the chart covers completely becomes a **Performed Track**: muted, its notes carried by Hit Objects, the player's presses the only thing that sounds it. A track the chart covers partially is not muted at all; it keeps playing in the **Backing** and the Hit Objects sound in unison with it, which the glossary calls a **Doubling**. There is no third state, and therefore no note that is neither performed nor sounded. What difficulty buys is not fewer notes in the song — it is the choice of which part you take responsibility for.

Key choices within that:

- **The Chart Level chooses the part, not just the density.** Because coverage is judged per track, fixing the part first and thinning second gives the musically backwards outcome: on a busy song, Easy under-covers the lead, so the lead is never muted and Easy is permanently decorative — while a simple counter-melody sitting right there could have been performed in full within the same budget. So the generator ranks candidate parts by prominence (is the instrument pitched at all, how high it sits relative to the others, how many distinct pitches it uses, how much of the song it is present for) and proposes the most prominent part it can **fully cover** at that level. Changing the level re-computes the proposal and visibly re-ticks it. Doubling the lead is the fallback when nothing fits, not the default.
- **The proposal is a proposal.** It arrives pre-ticked with a one-line reason and the user overrules it with a click, exactly as ADR-0012 settled for the Suggested Instrument. A heuristic that silently mutes the wrong instrument is the failure this feature is most likely to have, and the cheapest possible place to fix it is before generating.
- **Rating is derived, and it is not `difficulty`.** `difficulty` is a 1–10 authored setting that scales the judgment windows and nothing else; it says how forgiving the scoring is, not how hard the chart is. The generator needs the second number, so **Rating** is introduced as a 1–10 hardness _computed_ from a chart's Hit Objects — decaying per-lane **Strain**, peak-weighted, with chord width, hold overlap and hand imbalance as multipliers — for any chart, hand-made or generated. It is derived rather than stored precisely because a stored one goes stale the first time somebody drags a note, and a stale difficulty label is worse than none. Overwriting `difficulty` with it was rejected: it redefines a field every saved song already carries, and it removes the ability to practise a hard chart with lenient windows.
- **The Chart Level is a target, not a preset.** Each level owns a Rating band — Easy 1–3, Normal 3.5–6, Hard 6–7.9 — and the generator charts, rates and retunes until it lands in band. (Hard stops at 7.9, not 8, because the band test is inclusive at both ends: a band of 6–8 accepts a chart measured at exactly 8.0, which is inside the range this decision reserves. `rateChart` rounds to a tenth, so 7.9 is the whole step below the reservation and nothing lies between them.) A preset would make "Easy" a description of the settings rather than of the result, which on a dense song is simply false. The bands stop short of 8: **8–10 is unreachable by generation**, reserved for hand authoring, so a 9 always means a human made it and the generator is never judged on charts it was not trying to make. This is what makes the metrics load-bearing rather than cosmetic — the convergence loop is only as honest as the Rating is.
- **Hit Objects sit at the source notes' exact times.** The snap grid is uniform (`60000 / bpm / snapPoint`, `snapPoint ∈ {1,2,4,8,16}`) and has no triplet subdivision, and hand placement is snap-locked — so snapping would quantise triplets and tempo-changer columns onto cells they do not belong on, and drift every performed note up to half a cell against the Backing it is supposed to be part of. Exact placement costs the generated chart its grid alignment, which makes hand-editing a generated object slightly awkward (dragging one snaps it, and so moves it). We take that: the chart's job is to sound right.
- **Notes within ~30ms become one press.** A chord is a set on one Hit Object, so simultaneity is free; the window exists because a recorded performance's "simultaneous" notes are 10–20ms apart and would otherwise become near-simultaneous presses no hand can play, in violation of the lane invariant below.
- **A hold is a hold only where a hold is audible.** A note becomes a held Hit Object when the instrument's config declares `sustain` and the note is long enough — the same predicate MIDI import already gates on (`instrumentSupportsSustain`), for the same reason: a hold the engine plays as a tap is worse than no hold. It follows that dropping a sustain breaks coverage on a Cello and costs nothing on a DunDun, because on the DunDun the sample was a one-shot either way.
- **Generation never edits the open chart.** It creates and opens a new vsrg song. The vsrg composer has no undo — none, not a partial one — so an in-place generator would be a one-click unrecoverable overwrite of hand work. Re-rolling replaces the song this dialog just made and nothing else, which keeps "generate never destroys" true without leaving four near-identical songs in the library.
- **Tie-breaks are seeded.** Same source, level, lane count and seed give a byte-identical chart, so the generator can be pinned by golden fixtures the way every other conversion in this repo is, and a bad chart is always reproducible. Randomness breaks ties only; it never picks patterns, because the patterns are supposed to come from the music.

Considered and rejected: **merge, never drop** — every muted note lands in some Hit Object and easier levels merge more notes per press, which keeps the music byte-identical at every level and is genuinely attractive; rejected because merging is only lossless for notes that were already simultaneous, so past a certain density Easy either stops getting easier or starts turning runs into chords, and "the chart is faithful" quietly becomes "the chart is wrong in a new way". **Drop and accept holes** — the simplest generator by far and the most freedom to make a clean rhythm; rejected because deliberate silence at the easiest difficulty is the exact failure a miss is supposed to cause, and a beginner cannot tell the two apart. **Per-note backing**, extending the model so the Backing plays precisely what the chart does not cover — musically ideal, and it would make Chart Level completely free; rejected on scope and on durability: it needs a new concept in the saved format and a link between chart and backing that survives either being edited independently, which is a larger change than the feature that motivated it, and the audio song is a separate song by id. **Pure rhythm charts** with empty `notes` — no pitch-to-lane problem at all; rejected because it makes the chart independent of the song, which is the one thing this app's vsrg mode has that generic rhythm games do not. **Snapping to the grid** and **generator-chosen lane count** are rejected in the design spec with their own reasoning.

## Consequences

- **`getRenderableNotes` and the coverage rule now answer different questions and must not be conflated.** `hidden` controls what the composer timeline draws; `muted` controls what the player sounds. Coverage governs `muted` alone — a generated song leaves `hidden` untouched, so the source's notes stay visible above the chart that performs them.
- **The generator must set `bpm` and `pitch` from the source song.** `setAudioSong` never has: `vsrg.bpm` comes from the composer's own settings, so picking a background song today leaves the grid unaligned to the music it is drawn under. Nothing in this decision depends on the grid, but the snap points, breakpoint stepping and the composer's whole timeline do, and a generated song that ships with a mismatched bpm is unusable for the hand-editing pass that follows.
- **One VSRG track per charted source track, instrument copied.** Timbre is preserved by construction and Stranded Notes become impossible in generated output: the notes came from that instrument, so that instrument can voice them.
- **No two Hit Objects may occupy one Lane at one instant, across all tracks.** A keypress resolves through `renderableHitObjects.find(r => r.hitObject.index === key.index)` — one press consumes exactly one object, and a second at the same time and Lane is auto-missed however well it was played. This was always true and no hand author ever hit it; it is a hard constraint on the constraint pass, and the first thing a generated-chart validator should assert.
- **Rating exists with no corpus behind it.** The repo's only vsrg fixtures are two-Hit-Object goldens, so the 1–10 scale is defined by stated absolute anchors rather than fitted to real charts, and the anchors are a guess until charts exist to check them against. The bands are therefore the part of this decision most likely to move, and they are deliberately the cheapest part to move — Rating is derived, so re-tuning the curve re-rates every chart in the library at once, with no migration.
- **A Doubling has no coverage obligation, so it is free to be the better chart.** Where coverage fails the generator is unconstrained by fidelity and can place whatever plays best. That inverts the usual expectation — the mode that gave up on performing the part is the one with the most room — and is worth knowing before someone "fixes" it.
- **The composer still has no undo.** This decision routes around that rather than solving it; if undo ever arrives (ADR-0013 did the equivalent for the composed composer), generating in place becomes reasonable and the new-song rule should be revisited rather than preserved.
